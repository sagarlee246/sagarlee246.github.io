This project investigates the transport properties of asymmetric Al/AlOx/NbN Josephson junctions and compares them with symmetric Al/AlOx/Al junctions. The measurements were performed at a base temperature of 150 mK using both current-bias and voltage-bias configurations.

From the measured IV characteristics, key transport parameters were extracted, including the critical current $I_c$, the superconducting energy gap $\Delta / e$, and the normal-state resistance $R_n$. The symmetric junctions showed consistent energy gaps across devices, while the asymmetric junctions varied much more strongly from device to device.

Temperature-dependent measurements were also used to estimate $\Delta_{Al}$ and $\Delta_{NbN}$ and to check the critical temperature of aluminum. Subgap resistance measurements further provided estimates of the Dynes parameter for selected devices.

The overall result is a detailed experimental comparison of symmetric and asymmetric junction behavior, with several devices showing deviations from the simplest theoretical expectations.
