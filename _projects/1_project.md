This project studies how the new Quantum Training Lab can support teaching through a hands-on quantum Hall effect experiment in Nano3. I observed a student group during the experiment, interviewed them afterwards, and used their feedback to evaluate both the learning experience and the structure of the lab session.

Alongside the teaching study, I characterized the Hall sample itself. The analysis extracted an effective electron mass of $m^* = (0.088 \pm 0.001) m_0$, indicating that the electron wavefunction is mainly localized in the GaAs layer.

The main takeaway was that the experiment worked well as a teaching tool: students stayed engaged and developed a clearer intuition for the quantum Hall effect, but the workflow contains too much downtime to scale without restructuring.
